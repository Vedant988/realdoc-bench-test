## Norovirus cases in Mass. surged in 2024

By Camillo Fusone

**STAFF WRITER**

Mirroring a national trend, cases of the notorious stomach bug are surging in Massachusetts this winter, according to state data. The state reported 2,600 laboratory-confirmed cases of the virus in 2024 in preliminary data released last week by the Massachusetts Department of Public Health. That represents a jump of roughly 66 percent from the previous year, when the state reported 1,525 cases. Cases over the winter have so far outpaced the previous year, with 134 cases reported in November and 279 reported in December. In 2023, there were 52 cases reported in November and 112 in December.

### Officials say 66% jump in a year reflects US trend; call handwashing best defense

Public health officials say they have been monitoring the increase in cases.

Nationally, cases of the virus are surging in parts of the United States that winter, according to government data.

The most recent numbers from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention show there were 91 outbreaks of norovirus reported during the week of Dec. 5, up from 69 outbreaks the last week of November.

Numbers from the past few weeks show a maximum of outbreaks reported during that first week of December.

Norovirus symptoms include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal cramps, according to state health officials. Despite being one of the most common causes of foodborne illness, the virus is difficult to detect in a laboratory, and specialized norovirus tests are usually conducted only in certain circumstances.

A spokesperson for the OPHE said the uptick in cases may be attributed in part to more modern testing methods which can detect multiple pathogens at once. They also noted that certain test devices were recently found to be prone to false positives of norovirus.

People of all ages can get infected and fall sick from norovirus. Young children, older people, and those with weakened immune systems are most at risk, with dehydration from vomiting and diarrhea the top concern.
