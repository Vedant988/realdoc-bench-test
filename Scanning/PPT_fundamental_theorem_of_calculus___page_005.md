Generally, this is what we could do:

Area of 1 rectangle  $\approx f(x) \times h$

![Diagram of a single rectangle under a curve.](7319549a57ef6465410382f530e0250a_3_img.webp)

A diagram showing a single rectangle under a curve. The rectangle has a height labeled  $f(x)$  and a width labeled  $h$ . The curve is above the rectangle, and the rectangle's top edge is a horizontal line segment. The curve starts at the top-left corner of the rectangle and ends at the top-right corner, curving upwards.

Area of all the rectangles  $\approx \sum f(x) \times h$

Actual area under the curve  $= \lim_{h \rightarrow 0} \sum f(x) \times h$

which is written as  $\int f(x) dx$

( but evaluating this limit is incredibly complicated)
