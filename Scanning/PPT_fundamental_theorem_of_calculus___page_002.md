To estimate area  $A$   
under  $y = 3x^2$  from  
 $x = 2$  to  $x = 4$   
we could split it into small rectangles:

![Graph of y = 3x^2 from x = 2 to x = 4 with two rectangles.](83b4b3da6919b5fae1c97f4a027b2da7_2_img.webp)

A graph of the function  $y = 3x^2$  on a Cartesian coordinate system. The x-axis ranges from -4 to 4, and the y-axis ranges from 0 to 60. The curve is a parabola opening upwards, with its vertex at the origin (0,0). The area under the curve between  $x = 2$  and  $x = 4$  is shaded in black and labeled  $A$ . Two rectangles are drawn within this shaded area. The first rectangle has a width of 1 unit, starting at  $x = 2$  and ending at  $x = 3$ , with a height of 12 units (the value of the function at  $x = 2$ ). The second rectangle has a width of 1 unit, starting at  $x = 3$  and ending at  $x = 4$ , with a height of 48 units (the value of the function at  $x = 4$ ). These rectangles are an overestimate of the area  $A$ .

With 4 rectangles,  
the area  $\approx 48.96$  units<sup>2</sup>

![Graph of y = 3x^2 from x = 2 to x = 4 with four rectangles.](83b4b3da6919b5fae1c97f4a027b2da7_4_img.webp)

A graph of the function  $y = 3x^2$  on a Cartesian coordinate system, identical to the one above. The area under the curve between  $x = 2$  and  $x = 4$  is shaded in black. Four rectangles are drawn within this shaded area, each with a width of 0.5 units. The rectangles are drawn such that their heights are determined by the function values at the right endpoints of their respective sub-intervals. The heights of the rectangles are 12, 27, 48, and 75 units, corresponding to the x-values 2, 2.5, 3, and 3.5. These rectangles are an overestimate of the area  $A$ .
