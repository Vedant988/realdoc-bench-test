## Strong earthquake hits remote Tibet

Dozens killed as cold complicates rescue efforts

By David Pieron, Barry Wang, and Shadia Sharma  
NEW YORK TIMES

HONG KONG — Using their hands and shovels in frigid conditions, rescue workers dug through the rubble in the search for survivors after a deadly 7.1 magnitude earthquake toppled houses and joined people awake in a remote part of Tibet on Tuesday as the northern foothills of Mount Everest...

![A large photograph showing the aftermath of a powerful earthquake in a rural area of Tibet, with debris and damaged structures visible.](92fbfac6c1bf58a941ec0ae94268b258_9_img.webp)

First responders performed rescue work in Changsuo Township, Tibet, western China, on Tuesday.

At least 136 people have died and 148 were injured in the quake, which struck shortly after 9 a.m. at a depth of 6.2 miles in Dingchi County, near one of Tibet's most historic cities, in western China, state media reported. The quake was the country's deadliest since December 2023, when 151 people were killed in a 6.2 magnitude quake in the southwestern province of Gansu and Qinghai.

China's state broadcaster reported that more than 300 houses had experienced some form of damage in Dingchi County, where the average altitude is around 15,000 feet, among the Himalayas border with Nepal.

Rescue efforts were being conducted without heavy equipment, undermining the challenge of delivering resources to the largely isolated communities damaged by the quake. With temperatures in the region dipping as low as a degree Fahrenheit, rescue workers have a short window in which to locate survivors, who are not immediately clear how many residents had been left homeless.

Several earthquakes were felt in the area, including one in Nepal. The quake had a magnitude of 7.1, according to the U.S. Geological Survey, though it was measured as 6.8 by the China Earthquake Networks Center. The nearest town to the earthquake epicenter was Shigatse, the second-largest city in Tibet.

## Israel strikes kill at least 17 people in Gaza Strip

### Most women, children despite targeting Hamas

ASSOCIATED PRESS

Israel's air strikes on southern Gaza killed at least 17 people late Tuesday, nearly all of them women or children, the territory's Health Ministry and hospital officials said.

Five children were killed as they sheltered together in the same tent, said Ahmed Al-Kabra, director of the children's ward at nearby Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis. Their bodies were among the eight children and five women brought to the hospital after strikes on tents, homes, and a vehicle. Two bodies were unidentifiable.

The Israeli military said it targeted militants who had taken part in the Oct. 7, 2023, attack that sparked the war, without providing evidence. Israel said it took steps to lessen the risk of harming civilians and blamed Hamas for the civilian casualties.

#### France marks 10th anniversary of terror attack

PARIS — A decade after Islamic terrorists stormed the offices of the national newspaper Charlie Hebdo in a deadly assault that shook France to its core and ignited a global outcry in defense of freedom of speech, the nation paused on Tuesday to honor the victims and renew its resolve to fight for liberty and democracy.

#### Nine workers trapped in mine in northern India

GUWAHATI, India — At least nine workers are trapped inside a flooded coal mine in India's northeast. Army state officials said Tuesday an operation to free them is under way.

#### S. Korea court issues new arrest warrant for Yoon

SEOUL — South Korea's anticorruption agency said it issued a new court warrant on Tuesday to detain imprisoned former Yoon Suk Yeol after its previous attempt was blocked by the presidential security service last week.
