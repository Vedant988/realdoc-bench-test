## We should recall what prompted the Jay inquiry Harvey Proctor

**P**rofessor Alexis Jay, chair of the Independent Inquiry into Child Sexual Abuse (IICSA), declared her dismay yesterday at the 'politicisation of child sexual abuse'. While her observation is timely, it begs a fundamental question: where was her criticism of the politicians who relentlessly drove this inquiry into existence for their own ends? The origins of the IICSA are rooted not in impartial concern for justice but in the politically charged fervour whipped up by individuals such as Tom Watson, John Mann and Zac Goldsmith, three figures now elevated to the House of Lords. Each used their position to push falsehoods and fuel a national moral panic under the guise of exposing a so-called 'VIP paedophile network'. Watson, in particular, stands out for his questionable use of his parliamentary privilege. He peddled the lies of Carl Beebe, a convicted paedophile and paedophile whose lurid claims became the backbone of Operation Midland. Watson's baseless accusations tarnished the reputations of dedicated public service, including the D-Day hero Lord Bramall, falsely accusing him of having sex with a child as a child abuse. Theresa May, as home secretary, irresponsibly echoed this hysteria by stating in November 2014 that Operation Midland was merely 'the tip of the iceberg'. It wasn't even the tip of an ice cube.

**L**et us not forget that the IICSA was a product of political pressure, launched not to investigate grooming for answers to the fabricated claims of abuse at the hands of VIPs. When it collapsed, the inquiry scrambling to pivot its focus elsewhere, including grooming gangs — an area in which its work was neither comprehensive nor free from political undertones.

**I**f Jay is genuinely concerned about politicisation, the most troubling and uncomfortable reality that her inquiry, too was born from, is that the IICSA may have the wrong questions clear: it left key questions unanswered; chiefly, why Pakistani grooming gangs were allowed to operate with impunity for so long.
