A zero-order  $\lambda/2$ -plate mounted on a stepping motor precisely rotated the orientation of the incoming field with respect to the layer.

We placed a mount in front of the objective lens that allowed us to insert various beam shaping elements, such as beam stops of various size. The objective lens was a NA = 1.4 oil immersion lens (Leica 100 $\times$ , Planapo, Wetzlar, Germany). It is connected with a semiaperture angle of  $67.3^\circ$ , which is the highest for immersion lenses. Annuli were produced by obstructing the central part of the beam with a black circular foil attached to a plane parallel glass plate, as indicated in Figs 1 and 4. The diameters of the circular obstructions were 3.7, 4.5 and 5.0 mm, so that they amounted to 66, 80 and 89% of the diameter of the aperture, respectively.
